Hyperstor

hyperstorctl

Manages and tests a cluster through the cluster API. See Managing a cluster.

hyperstorctl --help lists the commands, one line each, and the options every command takes. hyperstorctl help COMMAND (or COMMAND --help) describes one command with the options that go with it, and help GROUP (help snapshot) lists a group’s commands. All of it is read from the two tables below, so what they say is what --help says. Run with no command, it prints the usage line, on standard error, with status 2.

CommandDoes
statusHealth (ok, warn or error) with its reasons, nodes and devices up, the raw space free and total over the devices in (before copies; - until devices have reported), and PG states and space per pool
nodesEach node and its devices: address, weight, free and total space (GiB; - until the device has reported to the leader), shard format (1, or 2 for v0.0.8’s; blank on a member that isn’t the leader, or from a node before v0.0.8; >2 when asked to convert), in or out
poolsEach pool: kind, copies, PGs clean, degraded, backfilling and inactive, and its usable free and total space (GiB, after copies or erasure coding)
pgsOne pool’s PGs with their acting and up sets; --pg N for one PG
pg-gridOne pool’s PG states, a character per PG, 64 to a line: . clean, d degraded, b backfilling, X inactive; with --usage, also a digit per PG for the data on its primary, 0 least to 9 most
perfEach device over the last minute: requests per second its PGs served as primary, MiB/s, the device’s own read and commit (write and flush) p99, the most I/Os it had in flight, its drive’s temperature and wear. With --device D, that device’s drive (model, firmware, PCIe link, NUMA node, write cache, SMART) and its last two minutes, 5 s a line. Asks the map service’s leader, which keeps 15 minutes; exit 1 for a device that hasn’t reported
top-volumesThe volumes sending most requests now, summed over their PGs (at most --limit, default 20): requests and MiB per second
adviseThe Advisor’s findings: a slow device, a device carrying a hot spot, a hot volume (with the move-volume that would move it to an NVMe pool), and what drives say of themselves (critical warnings, media errors, wear, heat, a PCIe link below its width or speed, a drive on another NUMA node than its reactor), each with the numbers behind it and what to do. It changes nothing
volumesOne pool’s volumes: id, name, size, state, tenant (- for none), realm, project (tenant/project), and the client instance that has it attached
create-volumeCreates a volume of --size in --pool, with --name and, optionally, --id, --tenant and --project; prints its id. A project names its tenant: with both, they must agree. A volume with a tenant and no project is in its tenant’s default project. A tenant’s volume is refused in a pool its realm may not use
delete-volumeMoves volume --id (with its snapshots) to the trash, where it stays for its pool’s grace period (a day by default); refused while it is attached, unless --force. Prints when it will be purged
snapshot listVolume --id’s snapshots: epoch, name, group and state
snapshot createTakes a snapshot of volume --id, named --name; an attached volume’s client is paused for it (at most 2 s, else the snapshot is abandoned)
snapshot deleteMoves the snapshot at --epoch of volume --id to the trash; refused while a clone or rollback depends on it
snapshot groupTakes a snapshot of every volume in --ids at one moment (a consistency group); prints the group and each snapshot
snapshot group-deleteMoves group --group’s snapshots to the trash; the group goes with the last of them
audit listThe audit log, newest first: every change and every refused call, by the map service and by hyperstor-mgmt for people; --since, --principal, --op and --limit (default 100) narrow it. Administrators only
audit settingsHow long audit records are kept; with --retention-days, sets it (0: forever)
trash listWhat’s in the trash, in --pool or every pool: volumes and snapshots, when each was deleted and when it will be purged
trash restoreBrings volume --id back (or its snapshot at --epoch), as --name if given, else under its own name, or NAME-restored if another volume took it meanwhile. Operators may
trash purgeDeletes volume --id (or its snapshot at --epoch) from the trash for good, at once, as an operation: prints its id; with --wait, follows it to the end
trash settingsShows --pool’s grace period and what a full pool does with its trash; with --grace or --on-full, sets them
scrub statusEach pool’s scrub, or --pool‘s: its interval and deep interval, how long since its PG scrubbed longest ago was scrubbed (OLDEST) and scrubbed deep (DEEPEST), the PGs overdue, with holes unchecked twice running, damaged or inconsistent, and what the last 7 days’ scrubs found. Up to a minute behind. Not cache pools
scrub pgs--pool’s PGs with their last light and deep scrub, how long ago, whether overdue, and what the newer one found; with --overdue, only the PGs overdue
scrub startScrubs --pool’s placement groups now, or only those given with --pg (repeat it for several), ahead of their turn; --deep reads every block. An operation, done once each PG has been scrubbed since it started; --wait SECS follows it; op cancel stops it, and the PGs not yet begun are dropped. Not for cache pools. Asked of a member that isn’t the leader, it goes on to the leader’s API (from member list) with the certificates of --tls or hyperstorctl.conf, or exits with status 3 and says why it couldn’t
scrub setChanges --pool’s scrub settings: --hours, --deep-every, --hole-repair, --deep-rate; those not given stay as they are. Storage nodes follow them from the next placement group. A change that checks less than the defaults (scrub off, more than 168 hours, deep every more than 7 passes, holes only reported, under 32 MiB/s) is refused unless --reason says why; the reason goes in the audit log, and health warns while the pool’s scrub is reduced. Through --mgmt, it needs a login of the last 10 minutes. Not for cache pools
clone-volumeCreates a volume from the snapshot at --epoch of volume --id, sharing its data, named --name, with --new-id or a random id. With --project N the clone is that project’s, which may be another tenant’s (a disk cloned from a shared image); its tenant’s realm must be allowed the parent’s pool
expand-volumeGrows volume --id to --size; never shrinks it. A client that has it attached sees the new size after it attaches again
write-volumeWrites --file into volume --id at --offset (default 0), in whole 4 KiB blocks (the file’s last block padded with zeros), holding the volume’s lease meanwhile: refused while another client has it attached. --sparse skips blocks of zeros, for a volume that reads zeros there (a new one). With --map, goes straight to the map service, without the API or a certificate
read-volumeReads --size bytes of volume --id at --offset into --out, in whole 4 KiB blocks; with --map as above
enrollMakes this client a management client of the cluster from a mgmt token in --file, or a scraper of the map service’s metrics from a metrics token: a key made here, the API (--api) checked against the root the token names; writes admin.crt, admin.key and root.crt into --out, as --tls reads them. The certificate lives 30 days: renew it with cert renew
rollback-volumeRolls volume --id (detached) back to its snapshot at --epoch
flatten-volumeCopies into clone --id what it reads from its parents, so they can be deleted, as an operation: prints its id; with --wait, follows it to the end
copy-volumeCopies volume --id into a new volume in pool --pool, named --name (default: the volume’s), with --new-id or a random id, as an operation: prints the new id and the operation’s; with --wait, follows it. With --epoch, copies that snapshot, while the volume stays in use; without it, the volume’s current data, which needs it detached (it is held meanwhile). The copy has no snapshots and no parents; holes and blocks of zeros aren’t written. It is listed while it fills, held by the copy, and deleted if the copy fails or is cancelled. Refused (exit 1) for a pool that isn’t there or is full, or an id in use
move-volumeMoves detached volume --id to pool --pool, keeping its id, as an operation: prints its id; with --wait, follows it. The volume is held while its data is copied, then switches pools, then its data in the old pool is deleted. Refused (exit 1) while it is attached, while it has snapshots (in the trash too: purge them), while a clone reads through it, for its own pool, and for a pool that isn’t there or is full. A clone is flattened by the move
outTakes a device out of placement: its PGs move to other devices
inBrings a device back into placement
weightKeeps --device’s placement weight at --weight W, or with --weight auto lets it follow the device’s capacity in TiB again. Its PGs move as the weight changes. Needs every storage node and map service member on a build that has it
full-ratiosThe fullness thresholds every device is held to, or with --nearfull, --backfillfull or --full sets those given. Past full a device refuses new objects and writes to unwritten blocks; past backfillfull it takes no backfill. Needs every storage node and map service member on a build that has it
device addAppends a device to --node N, served at --address A, of class --class C (default: that of the node’s other devices). It is device N × 1000 + i, at weight 0, so nothing moves until the node is restarted with one more --shard A=PATH:SIZE and the device reports its capacity. An address in use or a node that isn’t there exits 1
device removeRemoves --device D: takes it out, so its PGs move to the other devices of their pools, and starts an operation that removes it from the map once no PG lists it, with its node if it was the node’s last. Neither id is given again; the device’s data is left as it is. Refused (exit 1) when a pool using it would keep fewer copies than its min, or, without --force, than its copies, for want of failure domains; and when a device taking its data would pass the full ratio (95% by default, see full-ratios). Prints the PGs that move, pools left short and the space after. Then drop its --shard from the node’s options: a node started with it stops, saying so. op cancel brings it back in
device upgradeAsks for --device D, or with --all every device, to be converted to v0.0.8’s shard format; each node converts its own the next time it starts. One-way: builds before v0.0.8 can’t open a converted device. Refused (exit 1) while any device asked hasn’t reported its format (its node runs a build before v0.0.8, is down, or hasn’t reported yet), and for a device not in the map. Devices already converted are listed as such
device classKeeps --device’s class at --class C, or with --class auto lets it follow the device’s kind (nvme, ssd, hdd, file) again. A class holds devices of one kind, and a class named after a kind only that kind; a device whose kind isn’t known yet (its node hasn’t run) is refused, exit 1. Pools limited to classes move its PGs
device endpointsSets --device‘s endpoints to --endpoints, replacing them: each NET=IP:PORT on a data network of the cluster (see Several networks); none removes them. hyperstor-install keeps a joined host’s devices’ endpoints this way on every run, and gives each node the endpoints its devices have. A storage node’s certificate may set only its own devices’; refused (wrong network, bad address, another node’s device) exit 1
network listThe cluster’s networks: name, kind, whether trusted, and match. See Several networks
network setAdds network --name N, or changes it: --kind control|data|both, --match "subnet IP/LEN" or --match "ib PREFIX", and --trusted for plaintext after the handshake. While devices have endpoints on it, it can’t become control or change its match. Hosts take endpoints on it at their installer’s next run. A bad name, kind or match, or a network in use, exits 1
network removeRemoves network --name N; refused (exit 1) while devices have endpoints on it
port listEvery node’s network ports, or with --node N that node’s: each one’s role (*: set by the operator; else storage where it carries an endpoint, host otherwise), state and speed, traffic sent and received, the traffic that isn’t hyperstor’s, a lent port’s lease, the weight senders give its endpoints, and its addresses, from its node’s last reports (every 10 s) to the leader. See Several networks
port roleSets port --name NETDEV of --node N to --role host|uplink|storage|compute|converged|vm, or with --role default returns it to the default. Storage uses storage and converged ports, and a compute port only while it is lent: while its other traffic stays under 5% of the link for 60 s, taken back within 30 s when traffic appears. An unknown node or role exits 1
port lendingSets when compute ports are lent to storage, --lending off|idle|always, for the cluster, or with --node N --name NETDEV for one port; --lending default returns it to the default (idle for the cluster, the cluster’s for a port). idle lends a port once its other traffic stays under 5% of the link for 60 s; always lends it at once, in storage’s traffic class. A claim takes a lent port back whatever the setting
claim listThe live claims on ports, with their holder, mode and time left, and the lending settings; --holder H for one holder’s. An orchestrator’s certificate sees its own claims only
claim portClaims port --name NETDEV of --node N for --holder H for --ttl SECS (10 to 3600), shared, or with --exclusive so storage leaves it; a lent port is taken back either way. An orchestrator’s claims are held under its name (glidex: H). Refused (exit 1) while another holder’s claim on the port is live. An orchestrator’s certificate, or an administrator’s
claim renewKeeps --holder’s claim on the port for --ttl SECS more. Exit 1 when it has none, as after a lapse
claim releaseEnds --holder’s claim on the port, or with --force whoever holds it (administrators only)
pool createMakes pool --name N on devices of --classes C,C... (required): --kind replicated (default) or cache, --copies N (3; 1 for cache), --min N (half the copies, rounded up; 1 for cache), --pgs N (a power of two; default about 100 per device of its classes per copy), --chunk BYTES (4M), --domain device|host|rack (host). The map service doubles its PG count as devices are added, unless --autoscale off. A cache pool’s PGs wait --cache-grace SECS (60) for a down node before starting empty elsewhere. Refused (exit 1) for a name in use, classes whose devices span fewer domains than the copies, and hard disks with flash unless --force
pool setChanges pool --pool P: places its PGs on devices of --classes C,C... only, or with --classes any on every device; keeps a copy per --domain device|host|rack; renames it --name N; sets a cache pool’s --cache-grace SECS. What isn’t given stays; PGs move as for a weight change. Refused (exit 1) for .meta, for a name another pool has, when the pool’s devices would span fewer failure domains than its copies, and when the classes hold hard disks and flash both, unless --force, which warns. pools lists each pool’s classes, ! marking a forced mix. --pgs N, twice the pool’s PG count, splits its PGs in place, a few at a time, each paused for a moment; with --wait SECS, until it is done. The new PGs then move to their own devices a few at a time, once every PG of the pool is clean, and pools says how many are placed; refused (exit 1) for another count, for .meta, while the pool splits and until the last split’s PGs have moved. --autoscale on|off turns the autoscaler on or off for the pool; pools lists those with it off
pool copiesSets replicated pool --pool P’s copies to --copies N and the copies a write needs to --min M. More copies add a member to each placement group, which fills in the background while I/O goes on; fewer drop each group’s last-ranked copies at once. --dry-run lists the placement groups that change and each device’s fill after. Refused (exit 1) for .meta, cache and erasure-coded pools, for a --min outside 1 to the copies, when the pool’s devices span fewer failure domains than the copies, for a --min above the copies a placement group acts with now, and when a device would pass backfillfull. Needs step-up through hyperstor-mgmt
pool add-deviceLets pool --pool P use device --device D too, though it is not of the pool’s classes, or takes back a device removed from it; PGs move as for pool set, and it is refused as pool set is (exit 1), --force allowing hard disks with flash. A device serves every pool that uses it, sharing its space
pool remove-deviceStops pool --pool P using device --device D, whether one of its classes or added; its PGs there move to the pool’s other devices. Refused (exit 1) when the pool’s devices would span fewer failure domains than its copies. pools lists a pool’s classes, then +D for each device added and -D for each removed
pool deleteDeletes pool --pool P: it leaves the map at once, its id is never given again, and each device deletes its data within a minute or so. Refused (exit 1) for .meta and while the pool holds a volume, a snapshot or anything in the trash: delete and purge them first. --dry-run lists what it holds, deletes nothing, and exits 1 if it holds anything. --force deletes that first, as an operation: each volume, even attached (its holder is preempted), goes to the trash and is purged with its snapshots, clones before what they were cloned from, each audited; then the pool. Prints the volumes and the operation’s id; with --wait, follows it
io-testWrites random data to a new volume, reads it back and compares
drainTakes every device of --node out and follows its PGs as an operation, until none lives on the node; prints each PG that moves. With --dry-run, only prints what would move
token createMakes a single-use join token for --role node (a storage host), --role meta (a map service member), --role mgmt --name N (a management client such as glidex, named N: 1–32 of a–z, 0–9 and -) --role s3 --name N (an S3 gateway named N) or --role metrics --name N (a scraper such as Prometheus, which may read only the map service’s /metrics), valid --ttl seconds. Prints the token alone on standard output, and how to use it on standard error. Storage tokens are made on the map service’s leader
token listThe tokens this map service member made: id, role, expiry, and whether used, revoked or expired. Never the tokens themselves
token revokeMakes token --id unusable
s3 key createMakes an S3 access key for tenant --tenant N (1 or more), with an optional --description TEXT (up to 200 characters) and --project N, one of the tenant’s projects, where the buckets it creates go (default: the tenant’s default project). Prints the key’s id and secret on standard output, separated by a space: the secret is never shown again. The map service keeps it encrypted with the cluster key
s3 key listThe access keys: id, tenant, its tenant’s realm, when and by whom each was made, and its description. Never the secrets
s3 key revokeDeletes access key --id: gateways refuse it within seconds
s3 placement setSets placement --name N to keep new buckets’ objects in pool --data-pool NAME and their index in --index-pool NAME. CreateBucket uses default unless its LocationConstraint names another placement; until default is set, buckets can’t be created. Refused (exit 1) for a pool that doesn’t exist, a cache pool, or .meta. Kept by pool id: a renamed pool keeps its placements. Buckets made before keep their pools
s3 placement listEvery realm’s placements and their pools, by name and id; a pool deleted since shows as #ID (deleted)
s3 placement deleteRemoves placement --name N; buckets in its pools stay
s3 bucket listEvery tenant’s buckets as tenant/bucket (or one tenant’s, with --tenant N), with their realm, pools, objects, bytes (- for a bucket made before buckets were counted) and creation time; read from the store by the map service, so the storage path must be up
s3 bucket showBucket --name B of tenant --tenant N
s3 bucket deleteDeletes bucket --name B of tenant --tenant N, only if it holds no object and no upload under way; audited
tenant createMakes tenant --name N (1–63 of a–z, 0–9 and -; not default) and its project default; prints both ids. With --tenant ID, gives the record to a tenant number already used on volumes, buckets or keys; otherwise the tenant gets the next free number (tenants and projects)
tenant listThe tenants with records: name, id, realm, projects and glidex link
tenant renameRenames tenant --tenant ID to --name N
tenant deleteDeletes tenant --tenant ID. Refused (exit 1) while it has a project besides default, a volume (in the trash too), a bucket or an S3 key, or is in a realm other than default
project createMakes project --name N of tenant --tenant ID; prints its id, unique in the cluster
project listEvery tenant’s projects (or one tenant’s, with --tenant ID): name, id, tenant and state; (default) marks each tenant’s default project
project renameRenames project --project ID to --name N. A default project keeps its name
project moveMoves project --project ID to tenant --to N as an operation (prints its id; --wait SECS follows it): its buckets take the new tenant’s name, keeping their objects, then the project names its new tenant, and its volumes are that tenant’s. Refused first (exit 1) unless the new tenant’s realm may use every pool its volumes and buckets are in, the new tenant has none of its bucket names, and, across realms while hosts are bound to realms, none of its volumes is attached. Within one realm, someone with storage-admin on both tenants may move it; across realms, a cluster administrator. A tenant’s default project doesn’t move. The move can’t be cancelled: it ends moved, or undone if a bucket can’t move. --force passes the new tenant’s caps (cluster administrators; caps aren’t kept yet)
project deleteDeletes project --project ID. Refused while a volume (in the trash too) or bucket is in it; a default project goes with its tenant
admission offerOffers glidex tenant --tenant SLUG of directory --directory ISSUER (https://host/d/NAME) a place on this cluster (glidex directories): prints the offer’s id and its gxo_ token, once, for the tenant’s administrator to redeem at the directory (gxctl tenant opt-in --offer TOKEN). The token names mgmt at --url URL (https://host[:port], as the directory reaches it) and the SHA-256 of its certificate, --pin or read from the address (printed, to compare). Redeemed, the tenant is made here as --name N (default: the slug) in the directory’s realm: its own if trusted already, else --realm R, made if need be (default: the directory’s name). Lasts --days N (default 7, at most 30). People of the directory who sign in are sent back to mgmt at --ui URL (default: --url), the address their browsers reach it by. Refused (exit 1) when a local tenant has the name, or the realm is another directory’s
admission listThe offers: id, state (open, redeemed, revoked, expired), the glidex tenant and its name here, realm, expiry and directory
admission revokeRevokes open offer --id OFFER
admission directoriesThe trusted directories: issuer, id and realm; their feed’s revision and when the directory last answered; where people’s logins come back, and how old the feed may be before its people are refused; each admitted tenant and its tenant here; and what of the feed isn’t done yet (a name taken, a move refused), retried at every pull
admission endEnds the trust in tenant --tenant SLUG (or its glidex id) of directory --directory ISSUER (or its id): the tenant and its projects stay, unlinked, and mgmt tells the directory. With its last tenant, the directory is no longer trusted
admission stalenessHow long directory --directory ISSUER (or its id) may go unheard before its people are refused, --minutes N (1 to 10080): past that since its feed last answered, its sessions and tokens are refused, as a revocation may be missing; local logins work. --minutes 0: the cluster’s default, an hour (people from directories)
cluster set-operatorNames tenant --tenant ID, a tenant of a trusted directory, as the cluster’s operator tenant (glidex’s gxctl cluster set-operator); --tenant 0 names none
auth loginSigns a person of trusted directory --directory ISSUER in for the command line, by the directory’s device grant: prints the page to approve the login at, waits for it, and keeps the access token for this cluster and its refresh token in --token-file F (mode 0600); needs --mgmt URL, which names the cluster. --mgmt then reads the file and renews the token a minute before it expires. Over HTTPS, the directory’s certificate is checked against the host’s CAs, or --directory-pin
realm createMakes realm --name N (1–63 of a–z, 0–9 and -; not default), with an optional --description TEXT: a group of tenants with its own S3 gateway, keys and pools on the same cluster (realms)
realm listThe realms, default first: id, tenants, the pools whose lists name it, and its description; then the pools every realm may use
realm deleteDeletes realm --name N. Refused (exit 1) while a tenant is in it, a pool’s list names it or it has an S3 placement
realm tenantPuts tenant --tenant N in realm --realm R (default takes it back): its keys then work only at that realm’s gateways. Refused while any of its buckets or volumes is in a pool the realm may not use
realm poolSets the realms that may use pool --pool P: --realm R[,R...], or any for every realm (the default). Refused while a placement of a realm it leaves names the pool
realm hostBinds host --host H to realms --realm R[,R...] (add default for volumes with no tenant or in default), or any to unbind it: a bound host then attaches only volumes of those realms’ tenants, checked by the storage node holding each volume’s header. An S3 gateway is bound the same way, by the name in its certificate: it reads the keys of the realms it is bound to, and default’s alone while unbound. Until the data path’s mutual TLS, a host could give another name: binding stops mistakes, not a hostile host
realm clientBinds management client --client C (a mgmt identity, such as glidex’s datastore driver, by the name in its certificate) to realms --realm R[,R...], or any to unbind it: a bound client then acts only in those realms, as a person with roles there, and may still read the cluster’s status; an unbound one acts in the whole cluster.
op listRunning and recent operations (drains, flattens, copies, moves, purges), newest first; finished ones are kept a week
op getOperation --id

Through hyperstor-mgmt. With --mgmt, hyperstorctl is a person, not an administrator’s certificate: make an API token on the dashboard (or POST /auth/tokens), keep it in a file only you can read, and your roles decide what you may do. What needs a fresh login (deleting, rolling back, tokens, revocations, backups) is refused with (reauth_required): do it from the dashboard, or with the certificate. The map service’s audit names you (… for user:NAME).

With roles only in realms (storage-admin@acme), you reach only those realms: volumes and create-volume without --pool take the first pool your realm may use, and status is refused. volumes, trash list and op list name each row’s realm; create-volume names its --tenant unless default is your only realm. --in-realm R picks one realm, for you or for a cluster administrator who wants to see what that realm’s administrators see.

export HYPERSTOR_MGMT=https://the-map-service-host:8852
export HYPERSTOR_TOKEN="$(cat ~/.hyperstor-token)"
hyperstorctl status

Without --api, the address is $HYPERSTOR_API, else the api line of /etc/hyperstor/hyperstorctl.conf (written by hyperstor-install), else 127.0.0.1:8850. Likewise, without --tls the certificates are those of the tls line in that file, if any; with neither, it uses plain HTTP.

On the map service’s host, when neither --api nor --tls is given and the configuration’s socket line names a socket that exists, hyperstorctl uses that local socket: the kernel tells the map service who is calling, and root and members of the hyperstor-admin group may do everything. This keeps working when certificates have expired or are lost. Elsewhere it uses the API address and the administrator certificate, which root and hyperstor-admin can read.

io-test reaches the storage nodes as a client would: it asks the API where the map service is, then writes and reads through the client library. It prints the time and throughput of each phase.

A refused request prints the API’s message and its reason, a stable word for scripts such as (not_found), (token_used) or (not_leader); the cluster API lists them.

Exit statusMeaning
0Done; for status, health is ok. Also --help and help
1status: health is warn or error. Otherwise a failed request (no such device, pool or operation), a failed or cancelled operation, or a failed io-test
2A missing or malformed option, or certificates that can’t be read. The message names the argument or command, then comes the usage line
3The cluster API (or, for io-test, the map service) can’t be reached, or the map service member asked isn’t the leader
4Not allowed: no certificate, or one whose role may not do this
