# hyperstorctl

Manages and tests a cluster through the [cluster API](cluster-api.md).
See [Managing a cluster](../admin-guide/managing.md).

`hyperstorctl --help` lists the commands, one line each, and the options
every command takes. `hyperstorctl help COMMAND` (or `COMMAND --help`)
describes one command with the options that go with it, and `help GROUP`
(`help snapshot`) lists a group's commands. All of it is read from the two
tables below, so what they say is what `--help` says. Run with no command,
it prints the usage line, on standard error, with status 2.

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `status` | Health (`ok`, `warn` or `error`) with its reasons, nodes and devices up, the raw space free and total over the devices in (before copies; `-` until devices have reported), and PG states and space per pool |
| `nodes` | Each node and its devices: the node's host name (blank until the node has reported it, or from a node before v0.0.8), address, weight, free and total space (GiB; `-` until the device has reported to the leader), shard format (`1`, or `2` for v0.0.8's; blank on a member that isn't the leader, or from a node before v0.0.8; `>2` when asked to convert), in or out |
| `pools` | Each pool: kind, copies, PGs clean, degraded, backfilling and inactive, and its usable free and total space (GiB, after copies or erasure coding) |
| `pgs` | One pool's PGs with their acting and up sets; `--pg N` for one PG |
| `pg promote` | Last resort when a replicated PG's copies are lost (every acting member down for good): makes a live member of its up set hold it alone, so it serves again, giving up what the lost copies acknowledged since that member last had every write. For pool `--pool P` (required), PG `--pg N` or every PG whose copies are lost; the member `--device D`, or the only one holding anything, or the first of those holding the same. `--dry-run` prints each PG, what each member holds and what would be promoted; promoting needs `--force`. Exit 1 if a PG is refused: its members hold different copies (name one), one didn't answer, or none holds anything. Needs step-up through hyperstor-mgmt. See [Failures](../admin-guide/failures.md#when-the-last-copies-wont-return) |
| `pg-grid` | One pool's PG states, a character per PG, 64 to a line: `.` clean, `d` degraded, `b` backfilling, `X` inactive; with `--usage`, also a digit per PG for the data on its primary, `0` least to `9` most |
| `perf` | Each device over the last minute: requests per second its PGs served as primary, MiB/s, the device's own read and commit (write and flush) p99, the most I/Os it had in flight, its drive's temperature and wear. With `--device D`, that device's drive (model, firmware, PCIe link, NUMA node, write cache, SMART) and its last two minutes, 5 s a line. Asks the map service's leader, which keeps 15 minutes; exit 1 for a device that hasn't reported |
| `top-volumes` | The volumes sending most requests now, summed over their PGs (at most `--limit`, default 20): requests and MiB per second |
| `advise` | The Advisor's findings: a slow device, a device carrying a hot spot, a hot volume (with the `move-volume` that would move it to an NVMe pool), and what drives say of themselves (critical warnings, media errors, wear, heat, a PCIe link below its width or speed, a drive on another NUMA node than its reactor), each with the numbers behind it and what to do. It changes nothing |
| `volumes` | One pool's volumes: id, name, size, the bytes its head reads and those only its snapshots hold (one copy; `-` when the member asked doesn't lead), state, tenant (`-` for none), realm, project (`tenant/project`), and the client instance that has it attached |
| `space` | The space volumes use, in `--pool` or every pool: per tenant and per project in each pool, then per volume, in bytes of one copy beside the pool's copies. `HEAD` is what heads read, `SNAPSHOTS` what only snapshots (or clones through them) hold, `TRASH` every byte of volumes in the trash or being deleted, `USED` the three together; a clone's shared blocks count to the volume it was cloned from. From the primaries' counts of at most 30 s ago; the leader answers ([space](../admin-guide/managing.md#space)) |
| `create-volume` | Creates a volume of `--size` in `--pool`, with `--name` and, optionally, `--id`, `--tenant` and `--project`; prints its id. A project names its tenant: with both, they must agree. A volume with a tenant and no project is in its tenant's default project. A tenant's volume is refused in a pool its realm may not use. Without `--pool`, the pool the `pool` property of its project or tenant names ([datasets](../admin-guide/managing.md#datasets-and-their-properties)), else the first data pool. Its name is unique in its project: one another volume or bucket of the project has is refused (exit 1, `dataset_exists`); volumes in no project are not checked |
| `delete-volume` | Moves volume `--id` (with its snapshots) to the trash, where it stays for its pool's grace period (a day by default); refused while it is attached, unless `--force`. Prints when it will be purged |
| `snapshot list` | Volume `--id`'s snapshots: epoch, name, group and state |
| `snapshot create` | Takes a snapshot of volume `--id`, named `--name`; an attached volume's client is paused for it (at most 2 s, else the snapshot is abandoned) |
| `snapshot delete` | Moves the snapshot at `--epoch` of volume `--id` to the trash; refused while a clone or rollback depends on it |
| `snapshot group` | Takes a snapshot of every volume in `--ids` at one moment (a consistency group); prints the group and each snapshot |
| `snapshot group-delete` | Moves group `--group`'s snapshots to the trash; the group goes with the last of them |
| `audit list` | The audit log, newest first: every change and every refused call, by the map service and by `hyperstor-mgmt` for people; `--since`, `--principal`, `--op` and `--limit` (default 100) narrow it. Administrators only |
| `audit settings` | How long audit records are kept; with `--retention-days`, sets it (0: forever) |
| `trash list` | What's in the trash, in `--pool` or every pool: volumes and snapshots, when each was deleted and when it will be purged |
| `trash restore` | Brings volume `--id` back (or its snapshot at `--epoch`), as `--name` if given, else under its own name, or `NAME-restored` if another volume of its pool, or another dataset of its project, took it meanwhile. Operators may |
| `trash purge` | Deletes volume `--id` (or its snapshot at `--epoch`) from the trash for good, at once, as an operation: prints its id; with `--wait`, follows it to the end |
| `trash settings` | Shows `--pool`'s grace period and what a full pool does with its trash; with `--grace` or `--on-full`, sets them |
| `scrub status` | Each pool's scrub, or `--pool`'s: its interval and deep interval, how long since its PG scrubbed longest ago was scrubbed (`OLDEST`) and scrubbed deep (`DEEPEST`), the PGs overdue, with holes unchecked twice running, damaged or inconsistent, and what the last 7 days' scrubs found. Up to a minute behind. Not cache pools |
| `scrub pgs` | `--pool`'s PGs with their last light and deep scrub, how long ago, whether overdue, and what the newer one found; with `--overdue`, only the PGs overdue |
| `scrub start` | Scrubs `--pool`'s placement groups now, or only those given with `--pg` (repeat it for several), ahead of their turn; `--deep` reads every block. An operation, done once each PG has been scrubbed since it started; `--wait SECS` follows it; `op cancel` stops it, and the PGs not yet begun are dropped. Not for cache pools. Asked of a member that isn't the leader, it goes on to the leader's API (from `member list`) with the certificates of `--tls` or `hyperstorctl.conf`, or exits with status 3 and says why it couldn't |
| `scrub set` | Changes `--pool`'s scrub settings: `--hours`, `--deep-every`, `--hole-repair`, `--deep-rate`; those not given stay as they are. Storage nodes follow them from the next placement group. A change that checks less than the defaults (scrub off, more than 168 hours, deep every more than 7 passes, holes only reported, under 32 MiB/s) is refused unless `--reason` says why; the reason goes in the audit log, and health warns while the pool's scrub is reduced. Through `--mgmt`, it needs a login of the last 10 minutes. Not for cache pools |
| `clone-volume` | Creates a volume from the snapshot at `--epoch` of volume `--id`, sharing its data, named `--name`, with `--new-id` or a random id. With `--project N` the clone is that project's, which may be another tenant's (a disk cloned from a shared image); its tenant's realm must be allowed the parent's pool. A name another dataset of its project has is refused (`dataset_exists`) |
| `expand-volume` | Grows volume `--id` to `--size`; never shrinks it. A client that has it attached sees the new size after it attaches again |
| `write-volume` | Writes `--file` into volume `--id` at `--offset` (default 0), in whole 4 KiB blocks (the file's last block padded with zeros), holding the volume's lease meanwhile: refused while another client has it attached. `--sparse` skips blocks of zeros, for a volume that reads zeros there (a new one). With `--map`, goes straight to the map service, without the API or a certificate |
| `read-volume` | Reads `--size` bytes of volume `--id` at `--offset` into `--out`, in whole 4 KiB blocks; with `--map` as above |
| `enroll` | Makes this client a management client of the cluster from a `mgmt` token in `--file`, or a scraper of the map service's metrics from a `metrics` token: a key made here, the API (`--api`) checked against the root the token names; writes `admin.crt`, `admin.key` and `root.crt` into `--out`, as `--tls` reads them. The certificate lives 30 days: renew it with `cert renew` |
| `rollback-volume` | Rolls volume `--id` (detached) back to its snapshot at `--epoch` |
| `flatten-volume` | Copies into clone `--id` what it reads from its parents, so they can be deleted, as an operation: prints its id; with `--wait`, follows it to the end |
| `copy-volume` | Copies volume `--id` into a new volume in pool `--pool`, named `--name` (default: the volume's name with `-copy`, or `-copy-2`…, when it is in a project, whose names are unique; else the volume's), with `--new-id` or a random id, as an operation: prints the new id and the operation's; with `--wait`, follows it. With `--epoch`, copies that snapshot, while the volume stays in use; without it, the volume's current data, which needs it detached (it is held meanwhile). The copy has no snapshots and no parents; holes and blocks of zeros aren't written. It is listed while it fills, held by the copy, and deleted if the copy fails or is cancelled. Refused (exit 1) for a pool that isn't there or is `full`, or an id in use |
| `move-volume` | Moves detached volume `--id` to pool `--pool`, keeping its id, as an operation: prints its id; with `--wait`, follows it. The volume is held while its data is copied, then switches pools, then its data in the old pool is deleted. Refused (exit 1) while it is attached, while it has snapshots (in the trash too: purge them), while a clone reads through it, for its own pool, and for a pool that isn't there or is `full`. A clone is flattened by the move |
| `out` | Takes a device out of placement: its PGs move to other devices |
| `in` | Brings a device back into placement |
| `weight` | Keeps `--device`'s placement weight at `--weight W`, or with `--weight auto` lets it follow the device's capacity in TiB again. Its PGs move as the weight changes. Needs every storage node and map service member on a build that has it |
| `full-ratios` | The fullness thresholds every device is held to, or with `--nearfull`, `--backfillfull` or `--full` sets those given. Past `full` a device refuses new objects and writes to unwritten blocks; past `backfillfull` it takes no backfill. Needs every storage node and map service member on a build that has it |
| `device add` | Appends a device to `--node N`, served at `--address A`, of class `--class C` (default: that of the node's other devices). It is device `N × 1000 + i`, at weight 0, so nothing moves until the node is restarted with one more `--shard A=PATH:SIZE` and the device reports its capacity. An address in use or a node that isn't there exits 1 |
| `device remove` | Removes `--device D`: takes it out, so its PGs move to the other devices of their pools, and starts an operation that removes it from the map once no PG lists it, with its node if it was the node's last. Neither id is given again; the device's data is left as it is. Refused (exit 1) when a pool using it would keep fewer copies than its `min`, or, without `--force`, than its copies, for want of failure domains; and when a device taking its data would pass the `full` ratio (95% by default, see `full-ratios`). Prints the PGs that move, pools left short and the space after. Then drop its `--shard` from the node's options: a node started with it stops, saying so. `op cancel` brings it back in |
| `device upgrade` | Asks for `--device D`, or with `--all` every device, to be converted to v0.0.8's shard format; each node converts its own the next time it starts. One-way: builds before v0.0.8 can't open a converted device. Refused (exit 1) while any device asked hasn't reported its format (its node runs a build before v0.0.8, is down, or hasn't reported yet), and for a device not in the map. Devices already converted are listed as such |
| `device class` | Keeps `--device`'s class at `--class C`, or with `--class auto` lets it follow the device's kind (`nvme`, `ssd`, `hdd`, `file`) again. A class holds devices of one kind, and a class named after a kind only that kind; a device whose kind isn't known yet (its node hasn't run) is refused, exit 1. Pools limited to classes move its PGs |
| `device endpoints` | Sets `--device`'s endpoints to `--endpoints`, replacing them: each `NET=IP:PORT` on a data network of the cluster (see [Several networks](../admin-guide/planning.md#several-networks)); `none` removes them. `hyperstor-install` keeps a joined host's devices' endpoints this way on every run, and gives each node the endpoints its devices have. A storage node's certificate may set only its own devices'; refused (wrong network, bad address, another node's device) exit 1 |
| `network list` | The cluster's networks: name, kind, whether trusted, and match. See [Several networks](../admin-guide/planning.md#several-networks) |
| `network set` | Adds network `--name N`, or changes it: `--kind control\|data\|both`, `--match "subnet IP/LEN"` or `--match "ib PREFIX"`, and `--trusted` for plaintext after the handshake. While devices have endpoints on it, it can't become `control` or change its match. Hosts take endpoints on it at their installer's next run. A bad name, kind or match, or a network in use, exits 1 |
| `network remove` | Removes network `--name N`; refused (exit 1) while devices have endpoints on it |
| `port list` | Every node's network ports, or with `--node N` that node's: each one's role (`*`: set by the operator; else `storage` where it carries an endpoint, `host` otherwise), state and speed, traffic sent and received, the traffic that isn't hyperstor's, a lent port's lease, the weight senders give its endpoints, and its addresses, from its node's last reports (every 10 s) to the leader. See [Several networks](../admin-guide/planning.md#several-networks) |
| `port role` | Sets port `--name NETDEV` of `--node N` to `--role host\|uplink\|storage\|compute\|converged\|vm`, or with `--role default` returns it to the default. Storage uses `storage` and `converged` ports, and a `compute` port only while it is lent: while its other traffic stays under 5% of the link for 60 s, taken back within 30 s when traffic appears. An unknown node or role exits 1 |
| `port lending` | Sets when `compute` ports are lent to storage, `--lending off\|idle\|always`, for the cluster, or with `--node N --name NETDEV` for one port; `--lending default` returns it to the default (`idle` for the cluster, the cluster's for a port). `idle` lends a port once its other traffic stays under 5% of the link for 60 s; `always` lends it at once, in storage's traffic class. A claim takes a lent port back whatever the setting |
| `data-tls status` | Whether connections on the storage network authenticate with the cluster's certificates: the mode, `off`, `allow` or `require`, and whether sessions stay encrypted on networks not marked trusted. In `allow`, also the addresses storage nodes served without a certificate in the last ten minutes, with how many connections and which nodes: who `require` would cut off. Only the map service's leader holds that list; another member says to ask the leader |
| `data-tls set` | Sets that mode, `--mode off\|allow\|require`. In `allow`, a storage node, the map service or a client that holds a certificate starts TLS on each new connection, each end learns who the other is, and the connection is served by what its certificate's role allows; one that holds none is still served. In `require`, a connection that shows none gets only `Hello`, `Ping` and the start of TLS. `--mode require` is refused (exit 1), with the nodes named, unless the mode is `allow` and every storage node that is up holds a certificate and runs a release that refuses by it. Connections open already stay open; what they may send follows the mode at once. Each node counts the connections it serves unauthenticated (`hyperstor_unauthenticated_connections_total`) and those it refused (`hyperstor_refused_connections_total`). `--encrypt on\|off` also sets whether authenticated sessions stay encrypted, in kernel TLS, on networks not marked trusted and on addresses on no network; `--encrypt on` is refused (exit 1) in mode `off`, and while a storage node that is up runs a release that can't encrypt or doesn't keep an encrypted session (its kernel needs the `tls` module). Needs step-up through hyperstor-mgmt |
| `claim list` | The live claims on ports, with their holder, mode and time left, and the lending settings; `--holder H` for one holder's. An orchestrator's certificate sees its own claims only |
| `claim port` | Claims port `--name NETDEV` of `--node N` for `--holder H` for `--ttl SECS` (10 to 3600), shared, or with `--exclusive` so storage leaves it; a lent port is taken back either way. An orchestrator's claims are held under its name (`glidex: H`). Refused (exit 1) while another holder's claim on the port is live. An orchestrator's certificate, or an administrator's |
| `claim renew` | Keeps `--holder`'s claim on the port for `--ttl SECS` more. Exit 1 when it has none, as after a lapse |
| `claim release` | Ends `--holder`'s claim on the port, or with `--force` whoever holds it (administrators only) |
| `pool create` | Makes pool `--name N` on devices of `--classes C,C...` (required): `--kind replicated` (default) or `cache`, `--copies N` (3; 1 for cache), `--min N` (half the copies, rounded up; 1 for cache), `--pgs N` (a power of two; default about 100 per device of its classes per copy), `--chunk BYTES` (4M), `--domain device\|host\|rack` (host). The map service doubles its PG count as devices are added, unless `--autoscale off`. A cache pool's PGs wait `--cache-grace SECS` (60) for a down node before starting empty elsewhere. Refused (exit 1) for a name in use, classes whose devices span fewer domains than the copies, and hard disks with flash unless `--force` |
| `pool set` | Changes pool `--pool P`: places its PGs on devices of `--classes C,C...` only, or with `--classes any` on every device; keeps a copy per `--domain device\|host\|rack`; renames it `--name N`; sets a cache pool's `--cache-grace SECS`; with `--capabilities on` makes its storage nodes serve a client only what it has shown a sealed capability for (`off`, the default, serves as before). What isn't given stays; PGs move as for a weight change. Refused (exit 1) for `.meta`, for a name another pool has, when the pool's devices would span fewer failure domains than its copies, and when the classes hold hard disks and flash both, unless `--force`, which warns. `pools` lists each pool's classes, `!` marking a forced mix. `--pgs N`, twice the pool's PG count, splits its PGs in place, a few at a time, each paused for a moment; with `--wait SECS`, until it is done. The new PGs then move to their own devices a few at a time, once every PG of the pool is clean, and `pools` says how many are placed; refused (exit 1) for another count, for `.meta`, while the pool splits and until the last split's PGs have moved. `--autoscale on\|off` turns the autoscaler on or off for the pool; `pools` lists those with it off |
| `pool copies` | Sets replicated pool `--pool P`'s copies to `--copies N` and the copies a write needs to `--min M`. More copies add a member to each placement group, which fills in the background while I/O goes on; fewer drop each group's last-ranked copies at once. `--dry-run` lists the placement groups that change and each device's fill after. Refused (exit 1) for `.meta`, cache and erasure-coded pools, for a `--min` outside 1 to the copies, when the pool's devices span fewer failure domains than the copies, for a `--min` above the copies a placement group acts with now, and when a device would pass `backfillfull`. Needs step-up through hyperstor-mgmt |
| `pool add-device` | Lets pool `--pool P` use device `--device D` too, though it is not of the pool's classes, or takes back a device removed from it; PGs move as for `pool set`, and it is refused as `pool set` is (exit 1), `--force` allowing hard disks with flash. A device serves every pool that uses it, sharing its space |
| `pool remove-device` | Stops pool `--pool P` using device `--device D`, whether one of its classes or added; its PGs there move to the pool's other devices. Refused (exit 1) when the pool's devices would span fewer failure domains than its copies. `pools` lists a pool's classes, then `+D` for each device added and `-D` for each removed |
| `pool delete` | Deletes pool `--pool P`: it leaves the map at once, its id is never given again, and each device deletes its data within a minute or so. Refused (exit 1) for `.meta` and while the pool holds a volume, a snapshot or anything in the trash: delete and purge them first. `--dry-run` lists what it holds, deletes nothing, and exits 1 if it holds anything. `--force` deletes that first, as an operation: each volume, even attached (its holder is preempted), goes to the trash and is purged with its snapshots, clones before what they were cloned from, each audited; then the pool. Prints the volumes and the operation's id; with `--wait`, follows it |
| `io-test` | Writes random data to a new volume, reads it back and compares |
| `drain` | Takes every device of `--node` out and follows its PGs as an operation, until none lives on the node; prints each PG that moves. With `--dry-run`, only prints what would move |
| `token create` | Makes a single-use join token for `--role node` (a storage host), `--role meta` (a map service member), `--role mgmt --name N` (a management client such as glidex, named N: 1–32 of a–z, 0–9 and -) `--role s3 --name N` (an S3 gateway named N) or `--role metrics --name N` (a scraper such as Prometheus, which may read only the map service's `/metrics`), valid `--ttl` seconds. Prints the token alone on standard output, and how to use it on standard error. Storage tokens are made on the map service's leader |
| `token list` | The tokens this map service member made: id, role, expiry, and whether used, revoked or expired. Never the tokens themselves |
| `token revoke` | Makes token `--id` unusable |
| `s3 key create` | Makes an S3 access key for tenant `--tenant N` (1 or more), with an optional `--description TEXT` (up to 200 characters) and `--project N`, one of the tenant's projects, where the buckets it creates go (default: the tenant's default project). Prints the key's id and secret on standard output, separated by a space: the secret is never shown again. The map service keeps it encrypted with the cluster key |
| `s3 key list` | The access keys: id, tenant, its tenant's realm, when and by whom each was made, and its description. Never the secrets |
| `s3 key revoke` | Deletes access key `--id`: [gateways](hyperstor-s3.md) refuse it within seconds |
| `s3 placement set` | Sets placement `--name N` to keep new buckets' objects in pool `--data-pool NAME` and their index in `--index-pool NAME`. CreateBucket uses `default` unless its `LocationConstraint` names another placement; until `default` is set, buckets can't be created. Refused (exit 1) for a pool that doesn't exist, a cache pool, or `.meta`. Kept by pool id: a renamed pool keeps its placements. Buckets made before keep their pools |
| `s3 placement list` | Every realm's placements and their pools, by name and id; a pool deleted since shows as `#ID (deleted)` |

**Through hyperstor-mgmt.** With `--mgmt`, `hyperstorctl` is a person,
not an administrator's certificate: make an API token on the dashboard
(or `POST /auth/tokens`), keep it in a file only you can read, and your
roles decide what you may do. What needs a fresh login (deleting,
rolling back, tokens, revocations, backups) is refused with
`(reauth_required)`: do it from the dashboard, or with the certificate.
The map service's audit names you (`… for user:NAME`).

With roles only in realms (`storage-admin@acme`), you reach only those
realms: `volumes` and `create-volume` without `--pool` take the first
pool your realm may use, and `status` is refused. `volumes`, `trash
list` and `op list` name each row's realm; `create-volume` names its
`--tenant` unless `default` is your only realm. `--in-realm R`
picks one realm, for you or for a cluster administrator who wants to
see what that realm's administrators see.

```sh
export HYPERSTOR_MGMT=https://the-map-service-host:8852
export HYPERSTOR_TOKEN="$(cat ~/.hyperstor-token)"
hyperstorctl status
```

Without `--api`, the address is `$HYPERSTOR_API`, else the `api` line
of `/etc/hyperstor/hyperstorctl.conf` (written by
[hyperstor-install](hyperstor-install.md)), else `127.0.0.1:8850`. Likewise, without `--tls` the certificates are
those of the `tls` line in that file, if any; with neither, it uses
plain HTTP.

On the map service's host, when neither `--api` nor `--tls` is given and
the configuration's `socket` line names a socket that exists,
`hyperstorctl` uses that local socket: the kernel tells the map service
who is calling, and root and members of the `hyperstor-admin` group may
do everything. This keeps working when certificates have expired or
are lost. Elsewhere it uses the API address and the administrator
certificate, which root and `hyperstor-admin` can read.

`io-test` reaches the storage nodes as a client would: it asks the API
where the map service is, then writes and reads through the client
library. It prints the time and throughput of each phase, and the
networks it used.

`io-test`, `write-volume` and `read-volume` reach the storage nodes on
the same networks as the host's VM disks and S3 gateway: the installer
writes the host's client networks (its `--client-networks`) into the
`networks` line of `hyperstorctl.conf`. `--networks` names others, and
`--networks none` uses the devices' addresses. A network the cluster
doesn't have is warned about, and devices are reached at their
addresses on it. Without the line or the option (a host the installer
didn't set up), they use the devices' addresses.

A refused request prints the API's message and its reason, a stable
word for scripts such as `(not_found)`, `(token_used)` or
`(not_leader)`; the [cluster API](cluster-api.md#errors) lists them.

| Exit status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Done; for `status`, health is `ok`. Also `--help` and `help` |
| 1 | `status`: health is `warn` or `error`. Otherwise a failed request (no such device, pool or operation), a failed or cancelled operation, or a failed `io-test` |
| 2 | A missing or malformed option, or certificates that can't be read. The message names the argument or command, then comes the usage line |
| 3 | The cluster API (or, for `io-test`, the map service) can't be reached, or the map service member asked isn't the leader |
| 4 | Not allowed: no certificate, or one whose role may not do this |
