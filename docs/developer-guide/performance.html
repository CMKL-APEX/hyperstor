Hyperstor

Performance

These are the current key figures, from hyperstor-bench (see Measuring performance) and the timing tests. They are development measurements, not a product benchmark. Most cluster figures run every node on one host over loopback TCP; the figures on three hosts run over 1 GbE with devices that are files on a filesystem. No RDMA, NVMe passthrough or hard disk has been measured yet, nor a VM disk through hyperstor-vhost.

Paged checkpoints (host B)

2 GiB of 4 MiB chunks on a memory device, aged by a full pass of random 4 KiB overwrites, then overwrites to a share of the chunks (hyperstor-bench paged). Checkpoint traffic is scaled to a 1 TiB shard taking 50,000 writes a second:

Chunks writtenWhole checkpointsPaged, 128 KiB pages
All207 MB/s211 MB/s
A tenth207 MB/s86 MB/s
A hundredth207 MB/s13 MB/s
  • Uniform random writes gain nothing and pay about 2 % for page headers.
  • A volume’s chunks spread over every PG, so a hot tenth of the chunks still touches 40 % of the pages. Smaller pages helped little until 16 KiB, which cost 24 % more for uniform writes; 128 KiB stays the default.
  • Opening a shard (replaying its log) takes as long as before.

  • In the second run, 1 MiB reads held the reactor of host A’s consumer NVMe device for 100–153 ms at a time, which is why reads now wait for the device off the reactor. Those figures predate that change.
  • With commits off the reactor, no node logged a reactor hold, a slow commit or a device wait of 100 ms or more at 15,000 writes a second.
  • Under 1 MiB load the network dropped a few packets in a thousand, with round trips under 10 ms. Each lost TCP ping cost a 200 ms retransmission timeout, the cause of the occasional 300–400 ms silences that peers reported; pings now go over UDP.

These are that cluster’s numbers, not ublk’s: writes are bound by the files’ flushes on a shared disk (the same client path to memory devices does about 7,400 4 KiB writes a second at QD1), and the nodes and the client share the host’s cores. ublk runs one queue and copies data through the client for now. What it costs, and what a VM disk through hyperstor-vhost costs, needs a host with NVMe-backed nodes.

Findings that changed the design

FindingWhat changed
Deferring small writes cost about a third of batched write throughput on flash; end to end, redirecting gave 19 % more throughput and 2.6× lower p99Redirect-on-write on flash; deferral only for hard disks
Run maps grow to 33 GiB per TiB after random overwritesDense block maps
Writing a whole checkpoint at once stalled commits for 8–13 msFuzzy checkpoints in 64 KiB slices
Every checkpoint rewrote the whole index, however little changedPaged checkpoints: 16× less traffic when a hundredth of the chunks change
A copy-on-write B+tree committed per write costs 30–100× the metadata writes of the logThe planned B+tree keeps the log as its write-ahead log
Failover took the detection timeout plus about 110 msShorter leases and peer reports: about 1 s
Polling 200 µs after each request cut QD1 latency 15–23 % and a four-reactor p99 by 5×Reactors poll by default
Digest recovery sends 96× less than a full refill at 1 % changed, and digest lists are cheapNo PG log
One io_uring timeout per write cost about 3 % of write throughputPer-request deadlines on one 50 ms ticker per reactor
With four shared CPUs, cluster API load doubled QD1 read p99Control-path threads get cores of their own
A node whose reactor stalled past the peer timeout went down and up about 150 times a minuteFlap damping: a node is held down 2 s, doubling up to 60 s
Pings queued behind backfill pushes on the one connection to a peer, and an empty node’s refill flapped the clusterA 16 MiB backfill budget per target, and pings apart from data
A large key–value table held a reactor for 290 ms while digested, and opening its device took 1.4–4.4 sDigests in 1 MiB parts; the index copied at open only when there is a log to replay (1.4 s to 0.67 s)
Flushes of 105–212 ms on a shared filesystem held a reactor; under 1 MiB reads, so did readsThe engine waits for the device off the reactor, with I/O and read timeouts
A lost TCP ping waited 200 ms for a retransmission and delayed the pings behind itPings over UDP
Carrying a lease identity on every request costs nothing measurablePer-volume fencing on shared connections, instead of a client per volume

Not measured yet

  • io_uring against SPDK on the same NVMe drive.
  • RDMA, NVMe passthrough, and hard disks with a flash log.
  • VM disks through hyperstor-vhost, and the host read cache, on real hardware.
  • Clusters on networks faster than 1 GbE, and false failure detection there.
  • Client latency while a node is refilled.
  • Digest scans on PGs of hundreds of GiB.
